# Apple Levels and the Interview

Apple engineering levels run from ICT2 to ICT6, where ICT stands for individual contributor, technical. The loop keeps coding rounds at every level. Higher levels move the weight toward system design, ownership, and work across teams, and not toward harder algorithms.

These levels are commonly reported by employees and candidates. They are not an official Apple publication. Apple rarely shows the codes in public, and almost every engineer holds the plain title "Software Engineer".

## The ladder

| Level | Title | Typical scope |
|---|---|---|
| **ICT2** | Junior Software Engineer | Well-defined tasks inside one team, with regular review |
| **ICT3** | Software Engineer | Features built end to end with little supervision |
| **ICT4** | Senior Software Engineer | A project or problem area owned for one team |
| **ICT5** | Staff or Principal Engineer | Work that spans several teams |
| **ICT6** | Senior Principal or Distinguished Engineer | Direction across a whole product line or division |

The titles are the usual industry equivalents and not Apple job titles. ICT4 is the terminal level, which means an engineer can hold it for a whole career without pressure to be promoted.

## How the interview changes by level

| Level | Coding | System design | Domain and behavioral evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| ICT2 | The main signal; standard topics | Commonly reported as absent | Fundamentals and one project explained clearly |
| ICT3 | Still central | Common; a working design is expected | Independent delivery of a feature |
| ICT4 | Present; clean code is assumed | A deciding round; failures, user impact, and cost of each choice | Ownership of a project and its hard decisions |
| ICT5 | Present | Whether to build at all, boundaries between teams, rollout | Work led across several teams |
| ICT6 | Not commonly reported | Not commonly reported | Direction for a product area |

Senior and manager loops usually add a round with a director. That review is reported to concern the level more than the hire decision.

## What a senior answer adds

The reported difference between levels in the design round is specific. A mid-level answer is a design that works. A senior answer adds what breaks, what the user sees, the cost of each choice, and the option that was rejected. A staff answer adds whether the feature should be built, where team boundaries sit, and how the rollout proceeds.

The most common senior rejection is reported as a correct design given at the level below. The components and the interface are right, but the answer has no failure cases and no offline behavior.

## Leveling during hiring

- Apple is commonly reported to level external candidates conservatively. Down-leveling means receiving an offer one level below the level that the candidate expected.
- Engineers with four to five years of experience are often reported to start at ICT3.
- Recruiters usually share the target level when a candidate asks early.
- The level can be adjusted before the loop is set. It rarely changes after the interviews.
- Apple has five engineering levels where Google has eight, so each Apple level covers a wider range of experience.

Ask for the target level in the recruiter screen. Then give answers that show the scope of that level in every round. If you hold a competing offer, state its level plainly before the loop is scheduled.
