# The Apple Behavioral Interview

Apple has no single behavioral round with a fixed rubric. Behavioral questions are spread through the loop, from the recruiter screen to the final manager round, and engineers ask them as well as managers. The same four traits are commonly reported across teams: care for detail, strong opinions held with low ego, comfort with partial information, and real interest in the product.

## What Apple teams screen for

| Trait | What it means in an answer |
|---|---|
| Craft and attention to detail | You noticed a small defect that others missed, and you can say what fixing it achieved |
| Humility with high standards | You disagreed directly and with evidence, then supported the final decision |
| Comfort with partial information | You delivered your part of a project without seeing the whole plan |
| Product interest | You hold a specific opinion about a product, including what you would improve |

Apple also names values publicly, including privacy, accessibility, and simplicity. Candidates report questions about a feature that collects more data than it needs.

## Why secrecy shapes this round

Apple shares information on a need-to-know basis, which means that people learn only what their own work requires. Interviewers often describe the role in general terms and may not name the product. They ask how you work without the full picture, because employees often work that way after joining.

Asking precise questions about the team is welcome. Pressing a team for detail on unannounced work is reported to signal poor judgment.

## Question themes

**Motivation and product sense**

- Why Apple?
- What is your favorite Apple product, and how would you improve it?

**Craft and standards**

- Describe the project where you paid the most attention to detail.
- Tell me about a time you opposed shipping something because the quality was not there.

**Disagreement**

- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision. What did you do?
- Describe a serious disagreement with a colleague or manager.

**Ambiguity and failure**

- Tell me about a decision you made with incomplete information.
- Tell me about a time you failed. What changed afterward?

"Why Apple?" and "Tell me about a time you failed" are reported in almost every loop. The [Question bank](question-bank.md) has the full list.

## How to prepare stories

Use STAR, which stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. The situation and the task set the context in two sentences. The action is what you did yourself. The result is a measured outcome.

1. Prepare six stories that cover craft, failure, disagreement, ambiguity, and influence.
2. Put one specific detail in each story, such as the exact trade-off or the measured delay that you found.
3. Say "I" for your own actions. Team stories without your part are reported to score badly.
4. Explain why the better option was better for the person using the product.
5. For the failure story, name the specific behavior that you changed afterward.
6. Write your "Why Apple?" answer in advance, and say how you use the product that you name.

Two answers are reported to hurt candidates. The first is praise for Apple products without any detail of use. The second is a claim that you work best alone, because Apple teams depend on agreement across functions.

## Questions to ask at the end

Ask what the team works on as far as the interviewer can say, how decisions are made, and what the first six months look like. Avoid questions that the job posting already answers.
