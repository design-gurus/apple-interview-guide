Apple does not run one interview process for the whole company. Each team builds its own loop, writes its own questions, and makes its own hiring decision. Two candidates at the same level can therefore report very different interviews.

Three more things set the Apple loop apart. Most loops include a deep session on the candidate's own specialty, which this guide calls the domain round. The system design round treats privacy as a requirement equal to scale. Teams also keep product details private, so a candidate may interview without learning what the team builds.

This repository is a short, free guide to that loop. Each page takes about three minutes to read and ends with links to full lessons. The guide is independent. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by Apple, and every process detail is commonly reported by candidates and not an official statement.

## The loop at a glance

The stages below are commonly reported for engineering roles. The count and the order vary by team.

| Stage | Length | What it measures | Guide page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruiter screen | 20 to 30 minutes | Background, fit for one specific team, availability | [Interview process](interview-process.md) |
| Technical phone screen | 45 to 60 minutes | Working code and clear reasoning in a shared editor | [Coding interview](coding-interview.md) |
| Coding rounds in the loop | 45 to 60 minutes each, 1 to 3 sessions | Correct, readable code and handling of changed constraints | [Coding interview](coding-interview.md) |
| System design round | 45 to 60 minutes | Scale, trade-offs, user experience, and privacy | [System design interview](system-design-interview.md) |
| Domain round | About one session, sometimes more | Depth in your own specialty and in your resume projects | [Interview process](interview-process.md) |
| Behavioral questions | Spread through every round | Care for detail, disagreement, work with partial information | [Behavioral interview](behavioral-interview.md) |
| Final manager round | 30 to 60 minutes | Team fit and overall alignment | [Interview process](interview-process.md) |

## Frequently asked questions

### How many rounds are in an Apple interview?

Most engineering candidates report four to six main stages. These are a resume screen, a recruiter call, a technical phone screen, an onsite loop, and a final manager round. The loop itself usually contains four to six interviews, and some teams run more. See [Interview process](interview-process.md).

### How hard is the Apple interview?

The algorithm questions are commonly reported at medium difficulty, and unusual algorithms are rare. The difficulty comes from depth. An interviewer selects one line from your resume and asks several questions beneath it. Practice problems do not prepare you for that session, but a detailed review of your own projects does.

### How long does the Apple hiring process take?

Candidates report about four to six weeks once interviews begin. From application to offer, the full process often takes six to twelve weeks. A decision is commonly reported one to three weeks after the loop, because every interviewer must submit feedback first.

### What coding questions does Apple ask?

The reported topics are arrays, strings, hash maps, linked lists, trees, binary search, sorting, and simple dynamic programming. Some teams replace algorithm questions with problems from their own work, such as concurrency for a systems team. See [Coding interview](coding-interview.md) and the [Question bank](question-bank.md).

### Does Apple ask system design?

Yes, for mid-level and senior roles. Entry-level candidates are commonly reported to receive coding rounds instead. The questions usually resemble Apple products, such as file sync, media streaming, push notifications, and payments. See [System design interview](system-design-interview.md).

### Is the Apple interview the same for every team?

No. Each team designs its own loop, so the best source for your loop is your recruiter. Ask how many rounds there are, what each round covers, and which languages or platforms are expected.
