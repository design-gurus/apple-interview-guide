# The Apple Interview Process

Apple is commonly reported to run four to six main stages for engineering roles: a resume screen, a recruiter call, a technical phone screen, an onsite loop, and a final manager round. The whole process often takes six to twelve weeks. Each team builds its own loop, so treat every count on this page as a typical value and not a rule.

## The stages in order

| Stage | Format | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Resume screen | A recruiter reads your resume for one team | Whether your past work matches that team's work |
| Recruiter screen | A call of 20 to 30 minutes | Whether your background, interest, and availability fit the role |
| Technical phone screen | 45 to 60 minutes of live coding in a shared editor | Whether you write working code and explain your reasoning |
| Second conversation | Often with the hiring manager or a senior engineer | Whether your resume projects hold up under detailed questions |
| Onsite loop | 4 to 6 interviews, in one day or across several days | Coding, system design, domain depth, and behavior |
| Final manager round | A conversation of 30 to 60 minutes | Team fit and overall alignment |
| Debrief and offer | The panel meets, then the recruiter calls | Hire or no hire, and the level |

Senior and manager roles usually add a round with a director.

## What happens in each stage

**Technical phone screen.** Most candidates report CoderPad, a shared browser editor, and a free choice of language. See [Coding interview](coding-interview.md).

**Onsite loop.** The loop runs on site or by video. It mixes one to three coding sessions, a system design round for mid-level and senior candidates, and at least one domain round. Behavioral questions appear inside most sessions and are not saved for one interview.

**Domain round.** The interviewer selects one project from your resume and keeps asking why until you explain the mechanism. Expect questions one level below the code you write daily, such as how memory is laid out. An honest "I do not know, and here is how I would find out" is reported to score better than a confident guess.

## How the decision is made

Each interviewer writes notes and submits a rating. The panel then reviews them in a debrief, which is a meeting where the hiring manager reads all the feedback. The hiring manager owns the decision, and sources do not describe a central hiring committee. Strong doubt from one interviewer can stop an offer. For senior roles, a director also reviews the level.

## Timeline

| Period | Commonly reported length |
|---|---|
| Application to first recruiter contact | 1 to 3 weeks |
| First interview to end of loop | 4 to 6 weeks |
| End of loop to decision | 1 to 3 weeks |
| Application to offer | 6 to 12 weeks |

Offers are reported to arrive by phone and rejections by a short email without reasons. Recruiters rarely send status updates before a decision.

## Questions to ask your recruiter

- How many rounds does this team run, and in what order?
- What does each round cover?
- Which languages and platforms does the team expect?
- What is the target level for this role?

Recruiters at Apple usually answer these questions in detail, because each recruiter works with a specific team.

## After a rejection

Apple does not publish a waiting period for a new application. Candidates commonly report six to twelve months before applying again for a similar role. A different team is often open sooner.
